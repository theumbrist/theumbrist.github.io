HOUTHIS vs SAUDI ARABIA

Houthis claim strike on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery as Riyadh widens its coalition

The Houthis say they hit Ras Tanura, a 550,000 barrel-a-day refinery beside a major crude export terminal. Brent rose about 3% on the week, its first gain of November.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

On November 25 the Houthi movement said it had attacked Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, with a capacity of 550,000 barrels a day, sitting next to one of the world's biggest crude export terminals. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said fires broke out. Satellite imagery from NASA and Planet Labs showed thick black smoke over the site. The Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted and destroyed four drones in the Eastern Province and did not confirm that the refinery was hit. Three days later Saudi Arabia said it shot down a cruise missile aimed at Riyadh.REUTERS, CNN

The Houthis have fired at Saudi airports, oil infrastructure and office buildings since July, when Yemen's frozen war reignited as spillover from the US-Iran conflict. Riyadh's financial district has been among the targets. At least 26 people have been killed in suspected Houthi drone and missile attacks inside Saudi Arabia since September 23. The group is Iran-aligned, and Tehran is accused of supplying weapons and intelligence.CNN, AL JAZEERA

The asymmetry is in the price of a claim. Brent jumped 3.5% on the Ras Tanura reports, settling at $94.61, and closed the week around $95.44, up roughly 3% and its first weekly gain in November. Saudi Arabia carries the cost on the other side of the ledger: real GDP contracted 3.8% year on year in the third quarter, and Economy Minister Faisal Alibrahim told CNBC that Vision 2030 is moving from spending at any cost to spending at the right cost, with every riyal deployed with greater precision.REUTERS, GLOBE AND MAIL

Riyadh's answer is allies and air strikes. Ministers from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey met in Islamabad on November 27 under the Mecca defence agreement signed in August by Mohammed bin Salman, Shehbaz Sharif and Recep Tayyip Erdogan, which treats an attack on one as an attack on all and runs for ten years. Turkey completed ratification the same day. The three condemned the Houthi strikes. Houthi outlet Almasirah reported Saudi strikes on the Bir Abu Shamlah neighbourhood of Sanaa's Al-Tahrir district that damaged eight homes and three shops and wounded 13 people.AP, AL JAZEERA

Inside Yemen the ground war is moving in parallel. A government military spokesman, Colonel Majed al-Nuzaili, said forces carried out 726 attacks on Houthi positions in 24 hours and claimed 121 fighters killed or wounded, with more than six governorates struck overnight by the coalition. The UN says about 1.8 million people in government-held areas face emergency hunger, more than 300,000 have been displaced since July, and the Houthi encirclement of Taiz has created siege conditions.AL JAZEERA

Watch two things. Whether Saudi Aramco or the coalition ever concedes physical damage at Ras Tanura, which would turn a claimed strike into a measured supply loss, and whether the Mecca pact produces more than statements, such as Pakistani or Turkish air defence assets inside the kingdom. One reading is that the Houthis do not need to destroy Ras Tanura; the risk premium on Saudi oil and the squeeze on Vision 2030 budgets do the work. That sentence is interpretation, not a reported fact.CNBC, AP

UKRAINE vs RUSSIA

Zelensky Rejects Trump's "Friend" Framing for Putin as Kushner Praise Lands Badly

Trump says he asked Putin to pull back across Europe. Zelensky told the BBC that calling Putin a friend is like saying it about Hitler. The strikes continue through the diplomacy.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

Trump said he wants Vladimir Putin to pull back military aggression across Europe, and issued a joint statement with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte urging Russia to avoid steps that would widen the war in Ukraine. The statement followed their meeting in Florida. On Truth Social, Trump posted his own version of the text, which widened the warning to cover Ukraine as well as Russia.REUTERS, CNBC

The Washington line rests on personal access. Trump has called Putin his friend in public and posted a photo of the two of them with that caption after meeting him in China. The theory is that a direct channel to the Kremlin produces a deal faster than sanctions and arms deliveries have. The price is that Kyiv no longer reads the mediator as neutral.BBC, NBC NEWS

Zelensky answered on the BBC. He said Putin is an aggressor and a killer who cannot be a friend, and that the friendship language matches somebody praising Hitler during the Second World War. Days earlier Trump's envoy Jared Kushner had called Putin a historic figure and a grandmaster of politics, and described the war as a European proxy conflict. A Ukrainian lawmaker demanded his removal from the peace track and a German MEP called the remarks pure Kremlin propaganda.BBC, POLITICO EUROPE

The Kremlin inverts the charge. It accuses Ukraine of widening the war into the Mediterranean and the Baltic after Ukrainian naval drones sank a Russian corvette off Benghazi. Italy's prime minister also criticised Kyiv over that strike. Meanwhile Russia says it downed 1,246 Ukrainian drones in one night, Zelensky warns of a mass attack on the power grid within days, and Russia has extended its jet fuel export ban to March 2027 because drone hits on refineries keep cutting output.REUTERS, FINANCIAL TIMES

Two measures will show which track is real: whether Kushner stays as envoy, and whether the grid strike Zelensky described arrives. One reading is that the fuel export ban is the harder indicator, because it prices the damage Moscow cannot talk away.BLOOMBERG

BRIEFINGSat 28 Nov · 12:18Written briefing · 353 words

Hackers Breach Air Force Cloud One; Pentagon Stops Short of a Denial

Intruders got into the US Air Force's flagship cloud platform, which carries tactical, personnel and logistics data. The Pentagon says its networks are secure and will not address Cloud One itself.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

Hackers broke into Cloud One, the US Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, according to three sources cited by Reuters. The platform holds tactical, logistics and personnel data. Reuters reported the intrusion was found within roughly the past month, during work to upgrade the system, and that the discovery has caused disruption across the platform. Reporting says the intruders exploited a software vulnerability to reach the control plane. Who they are, how deep they went, and whether data left the system are all unestablished.REUTERS, DEFENSE NEWS

Cloud One is not a side system. It was started in 2017 as a single stop for Air Force IT, assembled from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet infrastructure. The applications running on it include personnel, logistics and maintenance tools, the Cloud-Based Command and Control system that pulls together air defense radar data, and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles across more than 200 Air Force locations worldwide.REUTERS, MILITARY.COM

That concentration is the point of the platform and also the exposure. Consolidation cuts the number of networks that have to be defended. It also means one software flaw sits upstream of hundreds of applications rather than one. One reading is that the Air Force traded a wide attack surface for a deep one, and this breach tests that trade.

The Pentagon's response was carefully bounded. A defense official told AFP that the department's systems are safe, secure and managed around the clock, and that mission-critical operations and applications would continue without disruption. An earlier statement said the department does not comment on the specific status of its networks, citing policy and operational security. Asked directly whether Cloud One had been compromised, officials did not deny it.FRANCE 24, AFP

The next pressure points are attribution and scope. Every day without a named intruder leaves allied air forces, which feed data into shared command and logistics systems, to assume the worst about what moved. Watch for three signals: a congressional demand for a classified briefing, forced credential resets or outages on Cloud One applications, and any shift from the current non-denial to a formal incident disclosure naming a state actor.REUTERS, DEFENSE NEWS

  • Hackers breached Cloud One, Air Force's flagship platform

US vs IRAN

US disables 15th blockade runner as Iranian-made drone debris kills two in Kuwait

American forces knocked out the propulsion of a Palau-flagged ship leaving Bandar Abbas. A day earlier, debris from intercepted drones killed two Italian technicians at a Kuwaiti radar site.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

US Central Command fired precision munitions at the Palau-flagged merchant ship Pacific Archer on November 27, destroying its propulsion after it left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas and ignored warnings to turn back. It is the 15th vessel publicly disabled since the blockade of Iranian ports began in April and the sixth since enforcement resumed in July. Another 158 merchant ships have been redirected without being hit.AP NEWS, WASHINGTON POST

The blockade started on April 13 and targets the route that carries about 80 percent of Iran's oil exports. A third US aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, has now reached the region, joining the George H.W. Bush and the George Washington. Trump says the war, in its ninth month, will end "whenever I want."AL JAZEERA, PBS NEWSHOUR

Iran's answer has been aimed at the Gulf states that host American forces. On November 26, drones were intercepted over an air-defence radar site in Kuwait. Falling debris killed two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall and wounded a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national. Iran's armed forces spokesman Abolfazl Shekarchi denied any Iranian operation there.BLOOMBERG, CNN

Italy's foreign minister Antonio Tajani told parliament the drone was Iranian-made and called the incident suspicious, saying it looked precooked. Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi repeated the denial to Tajani and to Kuwait's foreign minister and offered a joint investigation into where the drones came from. Iran's navy chief says the blockade proves "America has lost the war" and that Iran is already breaking it.CNBC, AL JAZEERA

The hard numbers cut against both claims of control. Tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz rose this month even as attacks increased, according to Vortexa data, while oil posted its first weekly gain of November on supply fears. Houthi forces hit Saudi Arabia's largest refinery complex at Ras Tanura days earlier, with satellite imagery showing smoke over roughly 600 square kilometres.GCAPTAIN, REUTERS

The signal to watch is whether Tehran moves from single runners to escorted convoys, and whether a strike on Gulf soil kills enough foreign nationals to pull a European government in. One reading, offered as interpretation: neither navy can close the other down at sea, so the pressure is transferred to the states in between, and Kuwait and Saudi Arabia absorb the damage first.AP NEWS

POLAND vs RUSSIA

Poland Asks NATO to Down Russian Missiles Over Ukraine, Plans Border No-Fly Zone

Warsaw wants allied aircraft to intercept Russian missiles while they are still in Ukrainian airspace. NATO has not agreed, so Poland is closing the airspace it controls instead.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

Poland has asked NATO allies to examine shooting down Russian missiles over Ukraine when they are heading toward alliance territory. Warsaw raised the idea at a meeting of NATO ambassadors in Brussels on 23 November. Some states backed it, the discussion was preliminary, and no decision was taken. In parallel, the Polish defence ministry announced a no-fly zone stretching more than ten kilometres inland from the eastern border, with the regulation intended to take effect in late December or early January.BLOOMBERG, NOTES FROM POLAND

The trigger was 20 November. Two missiles and a jet-powered drone came down on Polish soil during a Russian attack on western Ukraine, and investigators identified debris from Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles at all three sites. Further incursions were reported on 23 and 24 November. Poland then asked NATO for more air defence, including AWACS surveillance aircraft and F-35s.NOTES FROM POLAND, KYIV INDEPENDENT

Warsaw's argument is legal as much as military. Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said that downing Russian missiles and drones over Ukraine, at Ukraine's request, would fall within the definition of necessary defence. The no-fly zone serves a separate purpose: it supports a new procedure letting Polish pilots engage targets on radar, allied intelligence, altitude and speed, without visual identification.PAP, NOTES FROM POLAND

The alliance side is slower. NATO has not supported the proposal, pointing to the complications it creates and to earlier discussions that ruled out such an arrangement, including the risk of becoming a party to the war. Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said he sees willingness among allies to discuss the matter but, for now, no courage to act. Poland has also said allies declined to shoot down drones over Ukraine, citing numerous complications.BLOOMBERG, NOTES FROM POLAND

Moscow denies any part in it. The Russian ambassador in Warsaw, Georgy Mikhno, rejected the accusations as political judgements and unsubstantiated assumptions, and attacked what he called Russophobic rhetoric around the incident.NOTES FROM POLAND, UKRAINSKA PRAVDA

The asymmetry is the story. The no-fly zone is a Polish regulation and needs no consent. Intercepts over Ukraine need agreement from capitals that would carry none of the falling debris. One reading, as interpretation: Warsaw is building the part it controls while the rest stays blocked. The signal to watch is whether the zone enters force on schedule, and whether any ally publicly commits to firing outside NATO airspace.NOTES FROM POLAND, BLOOMBERG

  • No-fly zone extends ten kilometres inland

US vs CHINA

Trump Preps Tariffs on China, EU and 14 Others. Why Now?

Washington is finalizing new tariffs on sixteen trading partners, including China and the European Union, after a Section 301 investigation launched in March into so called excess industrial capacity. This briefing traces how a Supreme Cour

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

The Office of the US Trade Representative is finalising the results of an investigation into what it calls structural excess capacity. The probe opened in March and names 16 economies: China, the European Union, India, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Vietnam, Taiwan, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Switzerland, Norway, Singapore, Bangladesh and Cambodia. Reuters reports the findings and new duties are due imminently. The Wall Street Journal reports China's rate rises this week. Washington has not published the product lists.

The proposed ceilings are 15 percent for the EU, Japan and South Korea, and 19 percent for Malaysia. The existing 20 percent cap on Chinese goods holds only if the US-China trade truce is extended past January 10, 2027. If it lapses, the excess-capacity rate on China rises and stacks on tariffs already in force.

The legal route explains the timing. On February 20 the Supreme Court ruled that Trump could not impose tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, voiding the reciprocal and trafficking duties. In March, Jamieson Greer's office opened Section 301 investigations into close to 80 countries and economies. Section 301 requires a finding of unfair practice, and it carries no equivalent ceiling on rates or duration. All 16 economies in the capacity file already pay Section 301 forced-labour tariffs imposed in July.

Brussels denies the charge. The European Commission said in March that the sources of global overcapacity are identified and are not in Europe, and it calculates that 7 percent of EU exports already face duties above the 15 percent cap agreed with Washington last summer. It has promised a firm and proportional response to any breach. At the same time the Commission is drafting its own instrument to cut Chinese access to EU sectors during market disruption, due by the end of the year, and China's ambassador to the EU has threatened retaliation if it is used.

Two clocks now run. One is January 10 in Beijing's file, the other is the publication of the EU instrument in Brussels. The domestic constraint is visible: a post-midterm Reuters/Ipsos poll found 62 percent of Americans want the current tariffs repealed on cost grounds. Watch the product annexes when USTR publishes. They show whether the 15 percent cap is a ceiling or an opening bid.

At a glance

  • USTR probe covers 16 trading partners
  • Proposed caps hit 15 percent for EU
  • China truce lapses January 10 2027

AUSTRALIA vs CHINA

Solomon Islands Names Australia Primary Security Partner, Drops China Pact

A treaty signed in Canberra bars foreign military or security bases in the Solomon Islands, and Honiara has told Beijing it will not renew the 2022 policing pact that expires in April 2027.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

Anthony Albanese and Solomon Islands Prime Minister Matthew Wale signed a bilateral treaty in Canberra on 25 November. The text names Australia the Solomons' "primary security partner" and states that "there will be no foreign military or security base" on Solomon Islands territory. A day earlier, Honiara told Beijing it will not renew the security pact it signed with China in 2022, which expires in April 2027.REUTERS, NYT

Australia is paying for the arrangement. The package is A$740 million over ten years, made up of A$670 million in grants and A$70 million in concessional loans, covering policing, border security, health, education, resources and climate work, with the first money due to flow by January. Canberra will fund the expansion of the Solomons' police and border force. It is the same template Australia has already applied in Papua New Guinea, Vanuatu and Fiji.BUSINESS STANDARD, FRANCE 24/AFP

The motive is four years old. The 2022 pact with Beijing alarmed Canberra and Washington because its leaked text allowed Chinese security deployments and, in Australian readings, a possible quasi-military foothold. In practice China deployed a police liaison team of nine in January 2022, doubled it for the 2023 Pacific Games in Honiara, and kept roughly two dozen officers in the country providing weapons and martial-arts training.NYT, ABC NEWS AUSTRALIA

Beijing's response was procedural. Foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said China builds ties with the Solomon Islands on mutual respect and equality, and that its cooperation with Pacific states is not directed at any third party and is "not subject to influence from any third party." Beijing did not announce a counter-offer. Its remaining leverage is money: Australia supplies about 34 percent of the Solomons' overseas development finance, China about 19 percent, by Lowy Institute figures.GLOBAL TIMES, LOWY INSTITUTE PACIFIC AID MAP

One reading is that Beijing lost a document, not an installation: no base was ever built, so a lapse leaves nothing physical behind. The signal to watch is April 2027. Either the pact expires quietly, or China returns with a larger development package aimed at the same policing budget Canberra has just taken over. The second signal is domestic. Manasseh Sogavare, who signed the 2022 deal, now leads the opposition, and the treaty binds a government that elections can replace.ABC NEWS AUSTRALIA, YAHOO NEWS AUSTRALIA

US vs CHINA

Huawei convicted on 10 of 12 counts in Brooklyn sanctions trial

A US jury found Huawei guilty of trade secret theft, bank fraud and Iran sanctions violations, and acquitted it of racketeering. Sentencing is set for September 2027.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei Technologies and its subsidiaries on 10 of 12 criminal counts on 24 November. The verdicts cover conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud, wire fraud, sanctions violations and conspiracy to launder money. The trial ran 11 weeks. The jury acquitted Huawei of racketeering conspiracy and of conspiracy to obstruct justice, the two charges that framed the government's theory of the company as a criminal enterprise.CNBC, AL JAZEERA

The Justice Department's core claim was financial. Prosecutors said Huawei ran a business worth billions of dollars in Iran through Skycom, a unit it described to banks as an independent partner. Those misstatements, the government argued, turned sanctions evasion into fraud against the banks that cleared the payments. It is the same scheme that led to the 2018 arrest of Huawei finance chief Meng Wanzhou in Canada.BBC, REUTERS

Huawei said it will appeal. The company pointed to the racketeering acquittal, saying it confirms the allegations lack factual basis and vindicates the position it has held for eight years. Beijing has treated the case as political since the 2019 indictment, describing US action against Chinese firms as suppression and containment rather than law enforcement.EURONEWS, AL JAZEERA

The leverage sits outside the courtroom. Washington placed Huawei on the Commerce Department's Entity List in May 2019 over these same allegations, cutting it off from US chip technology. Huawei rebuilt a domestic chip and software supply chain instead. Revenue rose 9.6 percent in the first half of 2026 to about 69.6 billion dollars, and HarmonyOS now runs on more than 100 million devices.SCMP, NYT

Sentencing is scheduled for September 2027 before Judge Ann Donnelly. The government has not said what it will seek; fines, forfeiture and restitution could reach billions of dollars. The same week as the verdict, Washington blacklisted further Chinese chip-linked firms, including Megaspeed and an Alibaba unit in Singapore, amid a probe into Nvidia chip smuggling.CNBC, NIKKEI ASIA

One reading is that the fine is the smaller variable. The signals to watch are the post-trial motions and appeal filings before sentencing, whether Huawei seeks a negotiated resolution as Meng did in 2021, and whether the export-control list keeps expanding to Huawei's domestic suppliers. A penalty is a one-time number. Access to tooling is recurring.

  • Jury acquitted Huawei of racketeering charges

UKRAINE vs RUSSIA

Ukraine sinks Russian corvette in Libya, hits cargo ship in Gulf of Finland

Seven naval drones destroyed the corvette Merkury at Russia's Benghazi facility. A second strike burned the Russian-flagged Sparta II in the Baltic. Ukraine's naval war is now continental.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

Zelenskyy confirmed that Ukraine used seven naval drones to sink the Russian corvette Merkury at Russia's naval facility in Benghazi and to destroy a helicopter there. Two Libyan soldiers later died of injuries from the strike, according to a senior figure in Libya's eastern administration. Al Jazeera reported at least ten Russian and Libyan crew dead, citing local authorities, and said two of its sources described the ship as carrying weapons, ammunition and drones. Days earlier, drones set the Russian-flagged cargo ship Sparta II ablaze in the Gulf of Finland; Zelenskyy confirmed Ukrainian responsibility for that attack as well.AP NEWS, DEFENSE NEWS

Kyiv's stated logic is reach. Zelenskyy has said every vessel flying the Russian flag is now a legitimate target, and he framed the Baltic strike as part of a widening campaign of retaliation. The targets fit that frame. The Sparta II was sailing toward Kaliningrad and has been under US sanctions since 2022, with Ukraine adding it to its own list this year. Al Jazeera counted at least four Russian vessels targeted in November, among them the Merkury, the Angara and the Sparta II.REUTERS, MOSCOW TIMES

Benghazi is where the dependence sits. The port is in territory held by Khalifa Haftar, and it serves as Russia's transit hub for sustaining its military and political footprint across the Sahel and Central Africa. Without it, those operations run thousands of kilometres from home with fewer transit options. The eastern administration called the Ukrainian strike tantamount to a declaration of war. The UN support mission in Libya asked for an investigation. Haftar flew to Turkey days later, with Ankara pushing a mediating role between Libya's rival governments.AL JAZEERA, POLITICO EUROPE

Ukraine's own partners pulled back. Norway's defence minister Tore Sandvik said his government raised the need for stability in Norway's near areas with Ukrainian officials and told reporters that Ukraine must act within international law. Italy, a firm backer of Kyiv, warned the strikes could unsettle Libya's fragile civil-war balance. Putin said Ukraine keeps hitting Russian oil plants despite requests from Trump to stop, and promised an answer.REUTERS, POLITICO EUROPE

One reading is that Benghazi was treated as a rear area, assumed beyond Ukrainian range, and that assumption no longer holds. NPR reports the fighting has spread geographically in recent days, from the Libya sinking to missiles landing in Poland. The signals to watch are narrow: whether Russia moves its Mediterranean logistics to another port, whether Haftar demands Russian air defence for Benghazi, and whether Finland or Norway shift from private warnings to public limits on Ukrainian operations in Baltic waters.NPR, AL JAZEERA

NATO vs RUSSIA

NATO Vows Space Defense After German Warning on Russian Nuclear Satellite

German intelligence assessments put a Russian nuclear-armed satellite in orbit within 60 days. NATO says it will respond in space. Moscow calls the reports baseless.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

German public broadcasting reported on November 23 that Western intelligence services expect Russia to launch a satellite carrying a nuclear weapon, reportedly called Sputnik-S, as early as December 2026. The Financial Times reported Berlin's own assessment that the weapon could be in orbit within 60 days. A day later, NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte addressed the reports in public. The Kremlin rejected them.ARD, FINANCIAL TIMES

Berlin raised the alarm first, and the alliance followed within 24 hours. Asked about the German reports while in Berlin, Rutte said NATO would defend every inch of its territory and that this included safety in the space element. He said the alliance had a range of response options, some of which would not be visible to the public. The warning did not come out of nowhere: a U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency report released in October said Russia is developing a satellite designed to carry a nuclear weapon.BBC, DEFENSE NEWS

Moscow's answer was flat. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the report rested on so many alleged and possible claims, attributed to unnamed sources, that it was hardly worth taking seriously. Russia's embassy in Berlin called the account unfounded insinuation and part of a propaganda campaign, and said Russian space activity is peaceful and in line with international obligations.MOSCOW TIMES, BBC

The dependence runs one way. A nuclear detonation in orbit produces an electromagnetic pulse that can disable or degrade satellites across several orbital shells, including GPS, communications and military reconnaissance, with effects lasting months. The DIA assessment puts thousands of spacecraft at risk. NATO militaries and civilian infrastructure rely on those systems; Russia relies on them less. A weapon in orbit would also breach the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which Moscow inherited as the Soviet Union's successor.DW, DEFENSE NEWS

The report lands in a week of other friction. Russian cruise missiles came down in Poland, Russian warplanes prompted a rare shelter warning in South Korea, and Estonia closes all road border crossings with Russia on December 1 after an alleged sabotage plot. NATO officials describe this as one pressure campaign.NPR, CNBC

One reading is that the alliance spoke so fast because the assessment cannot be verified or refuted from outside, and silence would have left Berlin exposed. The signal to watch is narrow: any Russian heavy-lift launch in December with an undeclared payload, and whether NATO or Washington publishes tracking data instead of statements.

CHINA vs TAIWAN

China's Fourth Carrier Nears Launch as Taiwan Works on Ottawa

The nuclear-powered Type 004 could be in the water by 2027. Taipei's counter-move was a warning to Ottawa about the cost of leaning on Chinese trade.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

China's fourth aircraft carrier, the Type 004, is close to completion at the Dalian shipyard. Satellite imagery from October showed a hull of roughly 322 metres by 46 metres, larger than the three carriers already in service, and the ship is expected to be China's first nuclear-powered carrier. Analysts cited by Defense News and the South China Morning Post put a possible launch as early as 2027, because the build has reached the stage the carrier Fujian had in May 2021, about a year before it was launched in June 2022. Full operational service could take another five to ten years after launch.DEFENSE NEWS, SOUTH CHINA MORNING POST

Beijing is also working on what flies off the deck. State broadcaster CCTV said on November 12 that the J-35A stealth fighter is in line for incremental upgrades, including the full version of the WS-19 engine, rated at about 110 to 116 kilonewtons of thrust. Chinese analysts point to more internal weapons carriage and new radar-absorbent coatings, which appeared on images of the aircraft in August.GLOBAL TIMES, SOUTH CHINA MORNING POST

Taiwan's response this week was diplomatic. Deputy Foreign Minister Chen Ming-chi told The Canadian Press that Prime Minister Mark Carney risks exposing Canada to the economic coercion he has warned about, days after Carney met Xi Jinping in Shenzhen and agreed to further tariff reductions. Chen said China would leverage the new ties and weaponise interdependence, and flagged Chinese electric vehicles as a surveillance risk even if assembled in Canada.ASSOCIATED PRESS, TAIPEI TIMES

Taipei argued from its own ledger. The share of Taiwan's outbound investment going to China fell from more than 80 percent in 2010 to 3.8 percent last year, and exports to China and Hong Kong dropped from nearly 44 percent of the total in 2020 to about 27 percent. Taiwan's envoy in Ottawa has pitched an investment deal as a hedge. Carney has said Canada's trade relationship with Beijing is not the same as Washington's.ASSOCIATED PRESS, BNN BLOOMBERG

That is up by about two-thirds on last year. Amazon is near two hundred billion. Alphabet, one hundred and eighty-five. Four companies carry the load.

One reading of the timing is that a narrow, prosecutable question displaces a wide and unanswerable one. The political version ran in parallel. President Trump attributed a six-trillion-dollar fall in market value since the third of November to Democratic wins in the midterms, writing on Truth Social that he had forecast it. Two very different instruments, arriving in the same week.

We have seen this apparatus before, under its own name. Between June and August of 2015, a rally in Chinese equities built on margin debt reversed, and the Shanghai index lost more than two-fifths of its value over ten weeks. Beijing sent in state-owned institutions to buy shares directly: the China Securities Finance Corporation, Central Huijin, the foreign-exchange administration, state banks and brokerages. CNBC reported at the time that the programme ran past two hundred billion dollars, close to two percent of annual output, and ended up holding stakes in more than one thousand three hundred companies.

The follow-through is the part worth keeping. Prices steadied for a while. Then, in January 2016, China switched on a market-wide circuit breaker, and traders began selling into the threshold rather than waiting to be halted by it. The mechanism was suspended within days of launch. Research published later in the Pacific-Basin Finance Journal found the direct-purchase approach carried efficiency and moral-hazard costs without resolving the valuation problem it was built to contain. Chinese equities stayed subdued for years.

What is different now is where the shock begins. In 2015 the leverage was domestic and the bubble was domestic, so the state was containing a fire inside its own building. This time the same institutions and the same nine named funds are deployed at a larger scale than either 2015 or 2025, against a repricing that started in New York and arrived through the chip supply chain. A floor can hold a price. It cannot hold a capital-expenditure decision taken in Seattle or Menlo Park.

Two signals from the noise this week.

One. Nvidia is facing scrutiny on several fronts at once. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington wants answers on the company's chip sales to China, and wants to know who ran compliance. Reuters reported the SEC accounting inquiry. The Justice Department is also running an antitrust review of Nvidia's twelve-point-nine-billion-dollar Hugging Face purchase. Separately, China's commerce ministry called publicly for the revocation of a new US rule barring Chinese telecom firms from direct access to American phone numbers.

Two. Yemen's Houthis said they struck Saudi Arabia's Rabigh oil refinery, a new target in their campaign against shipping and infrastructure; CNBC reported the claim as President Trump warned of fresh strikes on Iran next year should diplomacy fail; Britain's maritime agency logged two tanker incidents off Oman and a vessel reportedly struck by a mine east of Hormuz; and Nikkei Asia reported crude holding in a narrow band, a risk premium intact, with global exports at a record high despite the Hormuz disruption.

So here is the question on the board. Every move this week bought time rather than resolution. Record purchases in Beijing. A third curb in a month in Taipei. An accounting inquiry in Washington, and a blame line on social media. Underneath them sits a spending wave of roughly six hundred and fifty billion dollars a year, concentrated in four companies. The defences are already running at record settings, simultaneously, across three markets. If the next leg down arrives before those capital-expenditure plans are revised, what instrument is still unused, and who is holding it?

  • Nasdaq fell $8.5 trillion since October 6

UKRAINE vs RUSSIA

Sea Drones Hit Russian Corvette Merkury at Libya's Benghazi Naval Base

Two of six drones struck a Russian warship docked for fuel and supplies in Benghazi on November 22. Libya's eastern government traced the launch site to a base run by Ukrainian forces in the west.

THE BRIEFING · FULL TEXT

A Russian navy corvette was hit by suspected Ukrainian sea drones at Benghazi on November 22, 2026. Six drones were launched at the vessel. Four were intercepted and destroyed. Two reached the ship, causing an explosion and a brief fire. Russian authorities had not commented on the blast that day, and no party claimed the attack.AL JAZEERA, FRANCE 24

Libya's eastern-based government said a preliminary investigation placed the explosion aboard a Russian warship that was docked for fuel and supplies. Libyan officials identified the vessel as a Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class corvette. Satellite imagery provider Vantor identified it as the Merkury.GCAPTAIN (REUTERS), AP NEWS

Imagery taken the same day showed most of the hull had capsized and sunk, with only the bow still above the waterline. Local sources told Al Jazeera the ship was moored inside the civil sea port, next to the naval base, and that an unknown number of Libyan and Russian nationals were killed at dawn. Those sources also said the cargo included weapons, ammunition and drones. Neither the casualty figure nor the cargo claim has been confirmed by the Russian or Libyan authorities.AL JAZEERA

The attribution is the part that changes the map. The eastern government's initial investigation found that the drones were launched from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya. The Associated Press cited unnamed sources to the same effect. Ukraine has not claimed responsibility.FRANCE 24, AP NEWS

Benghazi matters to Moscow because it is a transfer point. Russia has kept close relations with the eastern authorities under Khalifa Haftar and uses eastern Libyan ports as a logistics hub for its military operations in Africa and its naval presence in the Mediterranean. Fuel, spare parts and escorted cargo move through that corridor.AL JAZEERA

The loss of Tartus-level comfort in the Mediterranean has made those ports more valuable, not less. A warship that stops for fuel in Benghazi is a warship without a hardened naval home within reach. One reading is that Russia has been buying access with political support and arms deliveries, and access bought that way comes without air defence.AL JAZEERA

The Benghazi strike did not stand alone. A day earlier, in the Gulf of Finland, drones set fire to the Sparta II, a cargo ship bound for Kaliningrad and linked to Russia's armed forces. The vessel is under United States sanctions, and its owner, Oboronlogistika, provides logistics services to the Russian defence ministry.AL JAZEERA, FRANCE 24

That ship was damaged in international waters and then drifted into Finnish territory before leaving again. Finland's Defence Forces said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor. Finnish officials described the war as spilling over.AL JAZEERA, FRANCE 24

Two strikes in two days, one in the Baltic and one in North Africa, describe a campaign that no longer stops at the Black Sea. Sea drones are cheap, small and launched from the shore. They do not need a navy behind them. They need a coastline, a workshop and a target that sits still.AL JAZEERA, FRANCE 24

Who gains: Ukraine, which converts a low-cost weapon into pressure on Russian logistics far from the front, and does so without a formal claim. Who loses: the Russian navy, which now has to treat every refuelling stop as a defended position, and Haftar's administration, whose ports have become a battlefield. Libya's military said on November 23 that it was fully prepared after the Benghazi attacks.AL JAZEERA, FRANCE 24

Commercial shipping is exposed too. Al Jazeera reported that on the same evening a sea drone hit a Malta-flagged commercial vessel off Benghazi, a second target in the same series. Insurers price that kind of pattern quickly. Hull and war-risk cover for calls at eastern Libyan ports is the first place the cost will show.AL JAZEERA

What breaks next sits inside Libya. If the launch site in the west is confirmed, the eastern government has grounds to treat the Tripoli-based authorities as complicit, which pulls an external war into an unresolved internal one. One reading is that this is the most dangerous second-order effect of the strike, more than the loss of one corvette.FRANCE 24

Signals to watch: whether Moscow acknowledges the loss and names a culprit; whether Vantor or other imagery confirms the Merkury as a total loss; whether Russian supply runs shift from Benghazi to Tobruk, which has deeper water and is further from Tripoli; whether Ukraine keeps its silence on attribution; and whether Finland and other Baltic states adjust their rules for drone activity in their waters after the Sparta II case.AL JAZEERA, FRANCE 24

  • Attack traced to western Libya base
